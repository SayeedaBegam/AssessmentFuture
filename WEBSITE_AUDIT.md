# Assessment Futures website audit

This is the historical audit of the preceding version. The subsequent [content architecture migration](docs/CONTENT_MIGRATION.md) addresses the identity, index based metadata, and content validation debt described below. See [CONTENT_SCHEMA.md](docs/CONTENT_SCHEMA.md) for the current authoring contract; other audit findings should not be assumed resolved by that migration.

**Audit date:** 3 November 2026  
**Scope:** The supplied application source, local content, routes, components, styles, configuration, documentation, dependency lockfile, tests, and rendered application.  
**Environment:** Windows, portable Node.js 22.16.0, Next.js 16.4.4, React 19.3.1, local Chrome through Playwright.  
**Changes allowed:** Targeted bug and accessibility fixes. No rebuild, new major feature, dependency major upgrade, or major architecture/design change was made.

## Overall assessment

**Reviewer judgment:** This is a working local prototype with useful interactive tools. It can support a moderated usability discussion once the pilot boundaries, content review, accessibility checks, and environment described below are agreed. It should not be introduced as a validated assessment advisory service or a comprehensive research review.

The original tests passed, but additional audit tests reproduced five failures. Those failures were fixed and regression tested. Important remaining limitations concern the depth and review of the content, the small evidence collection, development dependency advisories, and readability of very small interface text.

### How to read this report

- **Verified:** Directly observed in source, a test, a rendered screen, a production response, or an identified original publication record. The method is stated where it matters.
- **Fixed:** An observed defect corrected during this audit and checked again.
- **Incomplete:** A verified limitation of the current implementation or corpus. It is not necessarily a broken control.
- **Not verified:** Something this review could not establish, such as institutional permission, full accessibility conformance, or pedagogical effectiveness.
- **Reviewer judgment / recommendation:** An interpretation or proposed next step, rather than a measured finding.

### Workload model checked

The default assumptions produce **28.0 hours**: 20 grading, 3 preparation, and 5 additional feedback hours. Switching to six group submissions per checkpoint produces **8.0 hours**. Adding ten minutes of oral verification for each of 30 students produces **13.0 hours**. These are calculations from entered assumptions, not observed lecturer workload.

Preparation is counted once; submissions and feedback are multiplied by marked checkpoints; oral verification is counted once per student. Ungraded feedback events, administration, moderation, resits, and scheduling overhead are not separately modeled. The interface explains these exclusions.

### Verified limitations remaining

- **The evidence collection is small and incomplete.** There are four real references, two reviewed publication page/abstract summaries, two entries awaiting full paper review, and **zero direct assessment evidence links**. All 18 assessment records currently receive the same two contextual references.
- **Five competencies have no assessment mappings:** Algorithmic reasoning, Mathematical understanding, Problem solving, Human AI interaction, and Human centered design. Selecting these competencies correctly returns no assessment results. This is a content coverage gap, not a search failure after F1.
- **Several filters offer little discrimination.** All assessment records have the same evidence availability value. Group size currently describes individual versus small group work, rather than cohort scale. Preparation has only two descriptive categories. These fields require editorial clarification before their apparent precision is trusted.
- **The knowledge map covers three of the 18 formats.** It is a real structured subset, not a complete map of the platform. Its fixed canvas requires horizontal scrolling on narrow screens; the working list alternative is important.
- **The playground is not a compatibility or validation engine.** It allows arbitrary competency, AI, resource, and format combinations. It assumes up to four students per group when collaborative work is selected. The new mismatch explanation helps, but does not establish disciplinary fit or institutional compliance.
- **Most practical adaptations and resources are shared templates.** The two adaptation patterns, the three downloadable templates, several accessibility prompts, and cross discipline examples are generic. Their presence should not imply that each of the 18 formats has undergone an independent research and implementation review.
- **State is not durable.** Designs, timeline edits, filter choices, and scenario snapshots clear on leaving/reloading their page. Downloads offer a way to retain some outputs. There is no import, autosave, or persistent project workspace, and none was added during this review.
- **Future integrations remain future work.** There is no authentication, CMS, database, approved AI service, German translation, feedback collection workflow, or deployed institutional host. No operational controls pretending to provide those services were found.

## Accessibility findings

### Verified strengths and fixes

- Semantic page headings and landmarks, a skip link, native form controls, labels, visible focus styles, and a Radix modal dialog are present.
- Dialog dismissal returns focus to the trigger. Tab arrow keys, Home/End, mobile navigation Escape handling, generated output focus, duplicate stage control names, and current navigation state were checked after fixes.
- Automated axe checks reported no violations under the configured WCAG 2 A/AA, 2.1 AA, and 2.2 AA tags on 11 representative light theme routes and five dark theme routes. Additional checks passed with the filter dialog open and with a generated mobile playground outline.
- Charts have a descriptive label and a semantic table containing the same scenario values. The map has a working list view. Formative/graded purpose is stated in text as well as color.
- Reduced motion is respected without the prior initial render mismatch. No blanket suppression of React hydration warnings was introduced as a fix.
- No full page horizontal overflow was found at 320 px and 768 px across seven representative routes, including a three format comparison. Existing 390 px checks across the wider route set also passed. Comparison tables, the semester overview, and map retain intentional internal scrolling.
- Actual desktop, mobile, tablet, and dark theme screenshots were inspected. Shared typography, color tokens, card treatments, spacing, and navigation are visually consistent in the inspected screens.

### Remaining limitations and recommendations

- **Verified usability risk:** Many labels and metadata lines use 6 to 11 px text. This is especially apparent on the mobile knowledge map. WCAG does not set one universal minimum font size, so the size alone is not being reported as a conformance failure. A readability pass with lecturers is still a priority.
- **Verified limitation:** The small screen graph exposes only part of its fixed canvas at once. The list view works, but graph scrolling and discoverability should be tested with actual participants.
- **Not verified:** Full screen reader behavior with NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver, or TalkBack; high contrast/forced colors; browser text-only resizing; touch use on real hardware; Firefox or Safari behavior; formal WCAG 2.2 AA conformance.
- **Not verified:** Whether large live regions in the graph and spectrum are too verbose, or whether focus after every possible deletion/reordering sequence is intuitive. Native browser automation cannot substitute for a screen reader session.
- **Recommendation:** Complete a focused manual keyboard and screen reader walkthrough, then test readability and zoom on actual participant devices. Do not describe zero axe findings as an accessibility certification.

## Security and privacy considerations

### Verified current behavior

- No student names, submissions, grades, course assignment files, lecturer profiles, or private university materials were found in the authored application source, content, configuration, documentation, or rendered pages reviewed. Research author names are public bibliographic data, not lecturer evaluations.
- No UTN logo or third party institutional logo is embedded. The mark is an original neutral SVG/CSS design. UTN and KI-Tutorien Bayern are mentioned as context; the site explicitly disclaims official endorsement.
- No API keys, credentials, application analytics SDK, user accounts, server write API, database, file upload, external AI request, or application data collection endpoint was found.
- Tool values stay in page memory. The theme is the only authored `localStorage` value. Downloads are initiated by the user. A production browser smoke run observed no requests to external origins in the home, workload, and playground flows tested.
- The only `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` use is a fixed theme initialization script; it does not interpolate URL or form values. Displayed content is otherwise rendered through React. This is a source review finding, not an exhaustive XSS penetration test.
- Development and production scripts bind to `127.0.0.1` by default. The existing preview was left available; the separate audit production server was temporary.

### Dependency findings

The full npm audit reported **19 affected package entries, including 17 moderate and two critical**, in the development toolchain. The critical entries are `tinypool` and its consuming `vitest` chain. Other findings involve test mocking and linting dependencies. Counts include inherited dependency entries and do not mean 19 independently demonstrated exploits.

Relevant advisory records include [Tinypool worker options](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-5gmw-xhrv-c9v3), [Tinypool run options](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-85c8-ppgw-ccpr), and [Vitest mock redirects](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-82fw-gwwq-j7x9). This audit verified the registry report, not exploitability under this application's exact local execution conditions.

The production only audit reported **zero** vulnerabilities. That is useful evidence about the dependency set at this point in time; it does not certify the application, Node runtime, operating system, or future package versions as secure.

No `npm audit fix --force` was run. Suggested fixes include major upgrades and, for some lint dependencies, disruptive major downgrades. A controlled toolchain update should be tested separately rather than treating registry suggestions as automatically safe.

### Before sharing beyond the local prototype

- **Verified gap:** The local production response did not include a Content Security Policy, frame protection, `X-Content-Type-Options`, or a Referrer Policy. No hosting layer is configured here. Choose and test appropriate headers with the eventual host; a strict CSP needs to account for the inline theme script and framework scripts.
- **Not verified:** Permission to present the project/institution names publicly, approved hosting, privacy notice requirements, data processing arrangements, or institutional approval. The absence of a logo does not establish permission for every form of project presentation.
- **Recommendation:** Use a controlled production build for remote participant access, keep development/test servers private, and review dependency/runtime patches and hosting controls before external use. Do not collect student work or personal teaching material during an initial usability pilot.
- **Scope limit:** Browser observations covered the tested flows, not every possible server/framework network operation. Next.js development activity and optional framework telemetry are separate from authored application data collection. The supplied launcher and audit commands disable Next telemetry, but deployment privacy settings still need review.

## Technical debt and maintainability

**Verified foundation:** Reusable TypeScript interfaces model assessments, papers, digests, competencies, programme contexts, adaptations, evidence links, scenarios, and resources. Content relationships resolve in tests. Pure workload/filter/design functions are separate from UI components. Shared buttons, dialog, shell, cards, and page headings support consistency. A lockfile, setup guide, content authoring guide, and tests are present.

**Verified debt:**

1. Assessment IDs/slugs are derived from display names. Renaming an entry can break bookmarks, comparisons, and relationships. Current uniqueness tests do not prove stability across future edits.
2. Important assessment metadata is assigned from array indexes, including purpose, AI category, and feedback. Reordering or inserting a record can silently change its meaning. Duration is also used to infer preparation category. Replace these assumptions with explicit reviewed fields before substantial content expansion.
3. Content schemas use many unrestricted strings and non-null assertions. There is no runtime validation, reviewer identity, source extraction trail, versioned approval record, or overdue review check.
4. Editorial text is mixed between components and data files. English is hardcoded, and there is no implemented locale dictionary or locale routing. The current code can be extended for German; it is not already localized.
5. The global stylesheet is approximately 5,060 lines, with repeated responsive and late refinement rules. It works in the tested screens, but future changes will require careful regression review. No stylesheet rewrite was attempted.
6. Knowledge map layout and selected content are fixed in code, including array-position references to assessments. It will not automatically scale to a larger corpus without layout and selection work.
7. Source verification uses a small status vocabulary that conflates bibliographic checks, abstract review, and full paper appraisal unless the prose is read. Add explicit review scope and provenance when the editorial workflow is improved.
8. Existing tests initially missed realistic search phrases, duplicate query parameters, fractional weeks, and AI selection handoff. Regression coverage has now been added, but no CI configuration, performance budget, cross browser matrix, or human accessibility review workflow is present in the supplied project.
9. Session state is intentionally transient. Whether lecturers need to retain and revisit a design is a product question for feedback, not a reason to add accounts or a database prematurely.

### Five actions required before lecturer feedback

1. **Review the pilot content and evidence claims**, keeping unreviewed sources and unvalidated adaptations unmistakable.
2. **Review competency coverage and planning assumptions**, especially the five unmapped competencies and generic format metadata.
3. **Complete a manual accessibility and readability walkthrough** with a screen reader, keyboard, zoom, and representative devices.
4. **Prepare a controlled, approved preview environment**, including a dependency advisory decision and confirmation of project presentation permissions.
5. **Agree a focused lecturer pilot brief**, with clear prototype limits, tasks, consent/recording arrangements, and no private student or course material.

These are readiness recommendations based on the findings above. They are not claims that pedagogical effectiveness, formal accessibility conformance, or institutional approval has already been established.
